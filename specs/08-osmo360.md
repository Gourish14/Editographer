# 08 — DJI Osmo 360 support

The app treats the DJI Osmo 360 as its primary camera: it detects Osmo files on upload, uses the camera's built-in proxies for fast analysis, and turns 360 footage into AI-directed virtual camera moves. Single-lens footage gets its own ultra-wide and high-frame-rate handling.

## What the camera records

| Mode | Resolution | Frame rates | `cameraMode` | How the app uses it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 360 · 8K | 7680 × 3840 | 24, 25, 30, 48, 50 | OSMO360_PANO_8K | Hero reframes, tiny planets, maximum detail per view |
| 360 · 6K | 6000 × 3000 | 24 to 60 | OSMO360_PANO_6K | Smooth 60 fps reframes and fast camera spins |
| 360 · 4K | 3840 × 1920 | 100 | OSMO360_PANO_4K100 | Native 4× slow-mo on drops |
| Single-lens · 5K | 155° ultra-wide | up to 60 | SINGLE_5K | First-person action shots |
| Single-lens · Boost 4K | 170° ultra-wide | up to 120 | SINGLE_BOOST_4K | Native slow-mo, extreme POV |
| Single-lens · 2.7K | 2688 × 1512 | up to 120 | SINGLE_2K7 | Fast action, slow-mo inserts |
| Anything else | — | — | FLAT | Normal flat video |

All modes can record 10-bit D-Log M at up to 170 Mbps. 360 footage is saved as `.OSV` with a low-resolution `.LRF` proxy; single-lens footage is HEVC MP4. Sources: [DJI launch](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/uk/dji/pressreleases/dji-revolutionizes-360-camera-market-with-the-osmo-360-3397954), [CineD specs](https://www.cined.com/?p=390295).

## Handling rules

1. **Detect.** `.OSV` or a 2:1 frame → 360; DJI metadata or 155°/170° lens → single-lens; fps 100/120 → native slow-mo source. Set `camera_mode` on the asset.
2. **Proxy analysis.** If the matching `.LRF` is uploaded with an `.OSV`, all analysis runs on the `.LRF`; the full file is used only at render. Upload the `.LRF` first: it is matched when the `.OSV` is analyzed. An `.LRF` is stored as a ready asset with no analysis job and is never used as a clip.
3. **Stitch.** Two accepted inputs:
   - Stitched equirectangular MP4 exported from the free DJI Studio / DJI Mimo (preferred: DJI stitch, HorizonSteady, invisible selfie stick).
   - Raw `.OSV`: each lens is projected on its own with FFmpeg's `v360` filter (`input=fisheye`, `output=equirect`) using the camera profile in `worker/app/adapters/osmo_calibration.json` (a shared lens FOV and a small yaw, pitch and roll for the second lens), then the two pictures are cross-faded across the overlap with a static mask (`maskedmerge`, 4° either side of each seam, all in 10-bit). The profile is picked by the `encoder` tag of the file ("Osmo 360", "Osmo 360 II"); an unknown camera gets the default profile. Only real lens streams count: the MJPEG cover picture ffprobe lists as a further video stream (index 7 or 9) is not a lens. The stitch runs once at analysis time and writes `media/stitched/{assetId}.mp4` (10-bit HEVC with audio); analysis and render both read that file. Both a two-stream and a side-by-side single-frame `.OSV` layout are handled.
     - Proven on two real files (T-414): an Osmo 360 and an Osmo 360 II clip. The lenses are about 194° each (193.97° and 194.41°, not the 195° first assumed). The second lens is turned by up to 1.7° from the nominal back-to-back mount, and by a different amount on each camera, so the profiles are per camera. On frames held out from the fit, the seam error across the overlap fell from 1.42° to 0.08° (Osmo 360) and from 0.58° to 0.19° (Osmo 360 II). That is 30 px to under 2 px, and 12 px to 4 px, at 8K. Objects closer than a few metres (the rider, the bike body) cannot line up at any setting: this is parallax, and the blend hides it as a soft join instead of a cut.
     - Not proven: other units of the same model (each unit has its own factory calibration in the file's metadata, which the app does not read), and exposure matching between the lenses (they differ by 0 to 12% at a seam; the blend smooths the step rather than removing it).
     - Cost: about 1.2 s per 8K frame on two CPU threads (the old single `dfisheye` pass took about 0.2 s), so a 23 s clip stitches in about 12 minutes of CPU time; this runs once per imported clip.
4. **Color.** D-Log M (detected from metadata or 10-bit HLG/log transfer) → DJI's official D-Log M to Rec.709 LUT first, then the style grade.
5. **360 analysis.** Each sampled frame is split into six cube faces (py360convert); YOLO, MediaPipe and scoring run on every face, so the brain knows where the action is in every direction. SAM 2.1 small tracks the chosen subject across the sphere.
   - **Subject to follow.** With a `followPoint` (the picker's tap), the render measures that subject before it frames the camera. The point belongs to the first 360 clip of the job. The planner runs once to find the source window each 360 segment uses, SAM 2 tracks the subject over that window (at most 90 s, forward and backward from the tap time, 4 samples per second), and the planner runs again with the measured track, which replaces the detection track. Segments, cuts and beats do not change between the two passes.
   - **Tracking view.** SAM 2 never sees the equirect frame: a person there is under 1% of its pixels, stretched, and cut in two when they cross the frame edge. It runs on a 100 degree rectilinear view centered on the subject, in chunks of 2 s. Each chunk re-centers the view on where the last one ended and prompts the subject at the view center, so the subject can cross the 0/360 seam or travel any distance. The mask center is mapped back to an equirect position. SAM 2 loads once per chunk through the GPU guard, so it is the only model on the GPU.
   - **Wrap.** Yaw is unwrapped before smoothing and keyframing, so a subject going from 359 to 1 degree moves the camera 2 degrees, never 358. Render wraps yaw per frame (rule 7).
   - **Fallbacks, never silent.** A tracker failure, or no subject at the point, keeps the detection track (the camera starts on the point) and adds a plan note. A subject lost mid-clip stops the track; the camera holds its last measured bearing, and the plan notes the time it was lost. Segments outside a 90 s window hold the nearest measured bearing, and the plan says so.
6. **Camera path.** The brain writes `camera360` keyframes (yaw, pitch, roll, FOV, projection) snapped to the beat (rules in `05-editing-brain.md` B7).
7. **Reframe render.** FFmpeg's `v360` filter (`adapters/ffmpeg_360.py`) projects the equirectangular frame to rectilinear, tiny planet or rabbit hole along the path, at the output ratio. Yaw, pitch, roll and FOV change per frame through `sendcmd` with `reset_rot=1`; a projection change splits one graph into sub-segments, because the projection is not a runtime option. Plan FOV is always a lens FOV: for a stereographic view it is converted so the image plane matches, `4 * atan(tan(fov / 2) / 2)`, so 160 becomes about 282 in `v360` and shows a small globe with no jump in scale at the switch.
8. **Resolution.** A view's pixel width = source width × FOV ÷ 360 (8K at FOV 100° ≈ 2130 px). Fast mode scales with Lanczos + sharpening; Max quality picks the Real-ESRGAN factor that reaches the target.
9. **Single-lens.** Ultra-wide look kept by default; the prompt "flatten" or the Cinematic style applies lens-distortion correction. A mid-recording front/rear lens switch is caught as a shot cut.
10. **High frame rate first.** Slow-mo slots prefer native 100/120 fps clips; RIFE only for normal-rate footage.

## 360-only effects

| Effect | What it looks like | When the brain uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject follow | Virtual camera locks on a person or vehicle anywhere in the sphere | Default for every 360 clip |
| Beat spin | 360° yaw whip around the subject in one beat | Drops and big hits |
| Tiny planet | World curls into a small globe | Intros, outros, section changes |
| Rabbit hole | Inverse tiny planet, sky becomes a tunnel | Builds before a drop |
| Planet-to-flat morph | Tiny planet unrolls into a normal view | Transition into the first drop |
| FOV punch | FOV snaps 140° → 70° | Downbeats, impacts |
| Barrel roll | Camera rolls 360° around the view axis | Fast action, jumps |
| Dolly zoom | FOV and virtual push in opposite directions | Dramatic slow sections |
| Look-around reveal | Smooth pan from subject to scenery | Travel and landscape shots |
| 360 hyperlapse | Fast forward with a steady heading | Long travel clips |

## Speed tips for the user (shown in the UI help)

- Fastest: single-lens 4K (no stitch, no reframe upscale for 4K output).
- For 360: export stitched from DJI Studio and upload the `.LRF` too.
- Use 8K 360 when you want the 360 moves, not for speed.
